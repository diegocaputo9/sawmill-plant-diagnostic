Visit file

Plant walk

Ten categories to look at and ask about on a walk. Click a category name to expand or collapse it at any time; you do not need to answer Yes, No, or Unclear first. For each category, mark whether you observed an issue, and choose Yes to open quick details. If you find the same kind of issue in another location, use + Add entry inside that category to record it separately. Check only what you actually saw or were told, and tick an evidence source only if it applies.

Process map

Name each main step in the order material flows. Open More details for the usual VSM data box items: unit, cycle time, operators, WIP before, changeover, and EPE. Everything except the name is optional. Map details (demand, shifts, available time, pace) are entered once per map so a VSM can be built after the visit. A flow chart is drawn automatically at the bottom as you enter the process steps.

Map details for the VSM (optional)

Picture of a drawn process map

Add a photo or image of a map drawn on paper or in another app. It is kept with this process map.

Flow chart

Built from the steps above, in order. An orange outline marks a step with a much longer cycle time than its neighbor, or the largest queue in front of the slowest step, only when the steps use the same unit. A dark outline marks the step you said sets the pace.

People and photos

Log what operators and supervisors tell you, and link each note to the area it concerns. Linked notes count as People evidence in the analysis. The photo log is a list only: take photos with your phone camera, then record them here.

Interview notes

Photo log

This is a log, not an upload. Take the photo with your camera, then record its number, where it was taken, and what it shows. Use the same number in Photo reference on the Plant walk.

Opportunities

Possible opportunities based only on what you entered. Each one shows two separate things: the evidence behind it (Observation, People, Data) and its effect on the line. Strong evidence of a small effect and thin evidence of a large one are not the same, so they are grouped differently. Treat single source items as leads to validate, not conclusions.

Second opinion from Claude

Sends your entries (not your photos) to Claude and asks for a review of the evidence: what looks supported, what is missing, and what to check before selecting a project.

Report

A plain text summary of the visit, the evidence, and the opportunities you marked as candidates.
